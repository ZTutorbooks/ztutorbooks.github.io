GOOD TO KNOW
  • 恭喜发财: In Cantonese it sounds like "Gung Hay Fat Choy!"
  • 恭贺新禧: Formal — often written on cards.
  • 年年有余: 余 (surplus) sounds like 鱼 (fish) — that's why fish is served at New Year.

GOOD TO KNOW
  • 红包: Given to children and unmarried young people. Avoid amounts with 4 — it sounds like "death."

  • 黄金万两: Often written as one combined "lucky character" on decorations.
  • 压岁钱: 岁 (age) sounds like 祟 (evil spirit) — the money "holds down" bad luck for children.

GOOD TO KNOW
  • 抓周: On a baby's first birthday, toys and objects such as a book, a brush and money are laid out. Whatever the baby grabs first is said to show their future.
  • 满月酒: When a baby is one month old, families hold a party to welcome the baby and thank the mother.
  • 红鸡蛋: Eggs dyed red are shared with friends and neighbors to announce a birth. Red is the color of joy.
  • 喜得千金: A daughter is called 千金, "a thousand pieces of gold", because she is so precious.
